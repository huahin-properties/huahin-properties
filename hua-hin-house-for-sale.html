Hua Hin Houses for Sale

Hua Hin is the most established second-home market in Thailand, roughly 200km and two and a half hours from Bangkok via Motorway 7. That drive time is the single reason the market works: it is short enough for genuine weekend use, which sustains demand from Bangkok buyers, retirees relocating from Europe and Scandinavia, and investors buying for holiday rental.

Choose the zone before you choose the house. Khao Takiab is closest to the beach and restaurants — highest price per square wah, easiest to rent, most liquid on resale. Thap Tai and Hin Lek Fai deliver noticeably more land and larger pools for the same budget, at the cost of a 15-minute drive into town; this is where families who intend to live here full-time usually end up. Central Hua Hin puts Bangkok Hospital, the international schools and the malls within minutes, which matters more than buyers expect. Soi 70 to Soi 112 has absorbed most new development in the last three years — modern layouts and good value, but a car is essential.

Four checks that protect your money. First, the title should be a Chanote (Nor Sor 4 Jor) with a completed survey, not a lesser deed such as Nor Sor 3 Gor. Second, confirm the access road is either public or formally registered as a servitude on the title — a verbal assurance from the seller is worth nothing when you come to resell. Third, establish whether water is mains supply from the provincial authority or a project borehole. Fourth, for any villa with a pool, get the annual pool maintenance and common-fee figures in writing before you sign.

Current price bands give a realistic starting frame: THB 3–5 million for a detached house in a Thap Tai estate; THB 6–12 million for a three-bedroom pool villa on 80–120 square wah; THB 15 million and above for sea-view or hillside luxury homes. Frequently asked questions

Can a foreigner buy a house in Hua Hin?

A foreigner may own the building outright but not the land in a personal name. The usual lawful routes are a registered 30-year land lease or ownership via a properly structured Thai company. Take independent legal advice before signing anything.

What are the transfer costs on a Hua Hin house?

Transfer fee of 2% of the assessed value, plus either Specific Business Tax at 3.3% (if held under five years) or stamp duty at 0.5%, plus withholding tax. Buyer and seller commonly split these 50/50 by agreement.

What rental yield do Hua Hin houses achieve?

Well-managed pool villas near the beach typically return net yields of 4–7% per year, driven mainly by high-season occupancy from November to February.
